# Content Playbook

**Product:** Revyu
**Version:** Draft v1
**Date:** 2026-09-18

Short-form content for building in public — personal audience, company
distribution, or both.

---

## 1. The honest starting position

**You have:** no product, no customers, no revenue, no audience.

**That rules out** most SaaS content advice, which assumes you have results to
show.

**But it hands you the one thing that works pre-launch:** a genuine unresolved
question with a deadline.

> *Do people actually scan QR codes on receipts and leave reviews? I'm going to
> find out in 60 days, and I'll show you the number either way.*

That is a real question, with a real answer coming, and a public commitment to
report it honestly. **People follow unresolved questions.** They do not follow
"here's what I'm building."

**Your unfair advantage:** you have already decided the number that kills this
(<5% conversion). Almost nobody does that publicly. Announce it, and every
subsequent video has stakes.

---

## 2. Pick a lane

Two audiences. They want different things and rarely overlap.

| | **Lane A — Builder** | **Lane B — Business owner** |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | Founders, devs, aspiring builders | Dentists, salon owners, gym owners |
| Content | Build journey, decisions, failures | Reviews, local SEO, getting customers |
| Serves | Your personal brand, hiring, future raises | **Direct lead generation** |
| Payoff | Slow, compounding | Faster, narrower |
| Risk | Audience never buys | Boring to make, harder to grow |

**Recommendation: Lane A now, Lane B from launch.**

Lane A is the only one you can make *today* — you have a build story and no
customer results. Lane B needs proof you do not have yet. Once you have three
clinics and real numbers, Lane B becomes your sales channel and Lane A keeps
running.

> ⚠️ **The trap:** builder audiences do not buy review tools. If Lane A grows
> to 50k and you have no customers, you have a following, not a business. Keep
> the kill metric as the scoreboard, not follower count.

---

## 3. Formats that work for this

| Format | Why it fits | Effort |
|---|---|---|
| **Talking head + screen recording** | Cheapest, most authentic, works for decisions | Low |
| **Number reveal** | "Day 30. Here's the conversion rate." Built-in tension. | Low |
| **Screen record + voiceover** | Show the actual product | Low |
| **Before/after** | Business's review count over time (post-launch) | Medium |
| **Whiteboard/diagram** | Explaining one concept | Medium |
| **Customer reaction** | Owner seeing their dashboard for the first time | High, best payoff |

**Do not:** over-edit, use stock footage, or fake a studio. Rough and real
outperforms polished for build-in-public.

## 5. Hook formulas that work

First 2 seconds decide everything.

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| **Number + stakes** | "6,000 lines of docs. Zero code. Here's why." |
| **Admission** | "I was wrong about my own product." |
| **Contrarian** | "I deliberately made my product worse." |
| **Countdown** | "Day 30. The number that decides everything." |
| **Warning** | "Every review tool you've seen breaks Google's rules." |
| **Curiosity gap** | "My AI refused to build this. It was right." |
| **Direct address** | "If you own a clinic, you're losing customers to this." |

**Avoid:** "Let me tell you about…", "So I've been building…", any 3-second
intro before the point.

---

## 6. Cadence — realistic while building solo

**Do not commit to daily.** You will stop, and stopping publicly is worse than
never starting.

| Phase | Cadence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Build (wks 1–3) | **2/week** | Sustainable alongside real work |
| Launch (wks 4–8) | **3/week** | Most interesting material |
| Post-decision | 2/week | Whatever the answer was |

**Batch:** film 3–4 in one sitting. The journal is your script source — that is
its second job.

---

## 7. The series spine

Individual videos get views. A **series** gets followers.

> **"Can I build a real business in 60 days? I wrote down the number that kills
> it before I started."**

- Every video is an episode
- Every episode ends closer to the answer
- The answer arrives on day 30 of installs
- **You publish it either way**

> **If the number comes back under 5%, publish that video.** A founder who
> publicly kills their own product on their own criteria earns more credibility
> than one who quietly pivots. It also becomes the most valuable video you make.

---

## 8. What NOT to post

| Don't | Why |
|---|---|
| Customer names without permission | Trust, and they are dentists with reputations |
| Real review content | The patient's words, not yours |
| Dashboard screenshots with real data | Blur or use demo data |
| Google policy specifics as legal advice | You are not a lawyer |
| Guaranteed results | Explicitly forbidden ([03-COMPLIANCE.md](03-COMPLIANCE.md)) |
| Competitor names when discussing gating | Defamation risk |
| Revenue claims you cannot evidence | |

> Sales constraints in [03-COMPLIANCE.md](03-COMPLIANCE.md) § Sales apply to
> content too. Never promise a number of reviews, a rating improvement, or a
> ranking improvement.

---

## 9. Measuring content

Vanity metrics lie. Track these:

| Metric | Why |
|---|---|
| **Saves / shares** | Real value signal, better than likes |
| Comments asking questions | Genuine interest |
| **Profile visits → website** | The actual funnel |
| **Signups attributed to content** | The only one that pays |
| Follower growth | Weakest signal — track, don't optimise |

**Add `source: 'content'`** to signup tracking so Lane B's value is measurable
rather than assumed.

> If content produces zero signups after 20 posts, it is a personal-brand
> project, not a growth channel. That is fine — just be honest about which one
> you are doing.

# 11. Full scripts — Hinglish

All 40, ready to shoot. Conventions:

- **[HOOK]** — first 2 seconds. Do not soften it, do not add an intro.
- **[B-ROLL]** — what to show on screen while you talk.
- **[CTA]** — closing line.
- `___` — **fill from [19-JOURNAL.md](19-JOURNAL.md).** Never invent a number.

**Delivery:** talk like you are explaining to a friend, not presenting. Hinglish
is natural code-switching — technical words stay English, emotion goes Hindi.
Do not force either direction.

**Length:** most are 30–45 seconds. Speak at normal pace; do not rush to fit.

> ⚠️ Scripts marked **TEMPLATE** describe things that have not happened yet.
> Fill the blanks from the journal. Do not invent specifics — fake detail is
> the one thing that kills build-in-public content.

### Script 1 — "6,000 lines of docs before any code"

**[HOOK]** Maine ek line code likhne se pehle 6,000 lines documentation likhi.
Log bolenge time waste — main aapko batata hoon usne kya bachaya.

**[B-ROLL]** Scroll through the documents folder, fast.

Teen decisions palat gaye. Sirf sochne se, build karne se nahi.

Pehla — maine socha tha QR hamesha chalta rahega, chahe customer paise de ya na
de. Likhte waqt samajh aaya: koi do mahine pay karega, cancel karega, aur
lifetime free reviews leta rahega. Poora business model wahin leak ho raha tha.

Dusra — maine socha tha QR sirf receipt pe jaayega, counter pe nahi. Baad mein
realise hua ki scan volume hi sabse bada risk hai. Rule badla.

Teesra — ek feature jo maine socha tha, compliance check mein fail ho gaya. Agar
build kar leta, mere customers ka Google profile khatre mein hota.

**[HOOK-BACK]** Agar main seedha code likhne baith jaata, teeno galtiyan
production mein milti. Tab fix karne ka cost 50x hota.

**[CTA]** Planning procrastination nahi hai — jab woh product badal de.

### Script 2 — "The number that will kill my startup"

**[HOOK]** Maine woh number likh diya hai jispe main apna startup band kar
doonga. Build karne se pehle.

**[B-ROLL]** Show the kill metric section in 07-METRICS.md.

Number hai **5 percent**.

Matlab — 100 log QR scan karte hain. Agar 5 se kam log poora flow complete karte

Koi pivot nahi. Koi "ek aur feature try karte hain" nahi.

**[HOOK-BACK]** Ye maine abhi likha hai — jab main emotionally attached nahi

**[CTA]** 30 din. 10 businesses. Number main public mein batauunga — chahe jo
bhi aaye.

### Script 3 — "My AI refused to build a feature"

**[HOOK]** Maine apne AI ko ek feature banane ko bola. Usne mana kar diya. Aur
woh sahi tha.

**[B-ROLL]** Screen recording of the conversation, blurred.

Maine kaha — agar customer 3 star se kam de, toh Google ka button mat dikhao.
Sirf humein feedback bhejo. Chhote businesses ke liye chupke se ye kar denge,
bade clients ko compliant version denge.

AI ne bola nahi. Reason ye tha —

Ye review gating hai. Google iske against actively enforce karta hai. Aur
penalty **mujh pe nahi, mere customer ke Google profile pe** aati hai. Reviews
delete, profile suspend, local search se bahar.

Aur sabse bada point — agar main ye chupke se karta, toh mere customers ko pata
hi nahi chalta ki unka risk kya hai. Woh decide hi nahi kar paate.

**[HOOK-BACK]** Maine argue bhi kiya. Bola user toh edit kar sakta hai. Phir bhi
mana kiya.

Aur ab jo version bana hai — woh better hai. Low rating pe private feedback
pehle dikhta hai, Google ka button usi screen pe, same size, ek tap. Kuch chupa
nahi hai. Aur zyada log private choose karte hain — kyunki naaraz log punish
nahi karna chahte, sunna chahte hain.

**[CTA]** Compliance ek constraint nahi hai. Woh moat hai.

### Script 4 — "Every review tool breaks Google's rules"

**[HOOK]** Jo bhi review tool aapne dekha hai — zyadatar Google ke rules tod
rahe hain. Aur penalty aapko milegi, unko nahi.

**[B-ROLL]** Simple diagram: happy → Google, unhappy → private form. Red X.

Ye kaam kaise karte hain — customer rating deta hai. Khush hai toh Google link
milta hai. Naaraz hai toh private form pe bhej dete hain.

Isko **review gating** kehte hain. Google explicitly isko prohibit karta hai.

Aur jab pakde jaate hain — tool company ko kuch nahi hota. **Aapka** profile
suspend hota hai. Aapke reviews delete hote hain. Aapka business local search se
gayab.

**[HOOK-BACK]** Agar aap koi review tool use kar rahe ho, ek cheez check karo —
1 star doge toh kya Google ka link dikhta hai? Agar nahi, toh aap risk pe ho.

**[CTA]** Main jo bana raha hoon usme har rating pe Google ka link dikhta hai.
Kyunki mujhe aapka profile bachana hai.

### Script 5 — "The one thing my product can't do"

**[HOOK]** Mere product mein ek problem hai jo main fix nahi kar sakta. Aur main
customers ko pehle hi bata raha hoon.

**[B-ROLL]** Phone screen — copy button, then Google's review page.

Customer review likhta hai mere app mein. Phir usko **copy** karna padta hai,
Google pe jaana padta hai, aur **paste** karna padta hai.

Kyun? Kyunki Google kisi ko bhi review pre-fill karne nahi deta. Koi API nahi,
koi link parameter nahi. Jo tool bolta hai woh ye kar sakta hai — woh ya jhooth
bol raha hai, ya kuch aisa kar raha hai jo nahi karna chahiye.

Mera estimate — yahan 40 se 60 percent log chhod denge.

**[HOOK-BACK]** Toh maine kya kiya? Chhupaya nahi. Screen pe pehle hi likha hai
— "aage Google aayega, wahan paste karna hai."

Kyunki surprise se log bhaagte hain. Expectation set karne se kuch ruk jaate
hain.

**[CTA]** Honest product banana slow hai. Lekin aap raat ko so paate ho.

### Script 6 — "I was wrong about my own product. Twice."

**[HOOK]** Apne hi product ke baare mein main do baar galat tha. Dono baar mujhe
kisi aur ne pakda.

**[B-ROLL]** Split screen — old doc text vs new.

**Pehli galti.** Maine design kiya tha ki QR hamesha chalega — paisa do ya na
do. Logic tha: printed QR customer ke haath mein hai, usko todna galat hai.

Problem — koi do mahine pay karega, cancel karega, aur hamesha ke liye free
reviews collect karta rahega. Apne Google profile pe khud check kar lega.

Ab kya hota hai — QR resolve hota hai, kuch toota nahi dikhta, lekin collect
kuch nahi karta. Pay karo, ek minute mein wapas chalu.

**Doosri galti.** Maine pricing tiers banaye the — alag niche, alag city, alag
price. Lagta tha clever hai.

Phir ye pakda gaya: **har tier mein features same hain.** Toh pricing page pe
teen numbers dikhaoge ek hi product ke liye? Customer poochega "main mehngi wali
mein kyun hoon?"

Aur do dentists ek hi sheher mein baat karenge. Ek ne 499 diya, ek ne 1299.
Reference base khatam.

**[CTA]** Apne ideas se pyaar mat karo. Unko test karo.

**[HOOK]** Sabne bola ek niche pe focus karo. Maine sabko allow kar diya. Aur

**[B-ROLL]** List of verticals on screen.

**[HOOK-BACK]** Toh solution kya rakha — har vertical ka data alag track

**[CTA]** Advice ignore karna theek hai. Cost pata na hona theek nahi.

### Script 10 — "The most dangerous phrase in startups"

**[HOOK]** Startups mein sabse khatarnak line — "baad mein add kar denge."

**[B-ROLL]** Database schema on screen.

Aaj maine ek column ka naam badla. `amount_paise` se `amount_minor`.

Chhoti si baat lagti hai. Lekin socho — agar main `paise` naam rakhta, toh jis
din doosre country ka customer aata, ye naam har query mein, har invoice mein,
har report mein galat ho jaata. Live payment records migrate karne padte.

Aaj ye badalne mein **ek ghanta** laga. Customers aane ke baad — hafte.

**[HOOK-BACK]** Lekin dhyan do — maine multi-currency **build nahi kiya.** Sirf
column ka naam sahi rakha aur currency field add kar diya.

Ye difference important hai. **Build global-ready, launch narrow.** Assumptions
mat bake karo — lekin features bhi mat banao jinki abhi zaroorat nahi.

**[CTA]** Jo cheez badalna mehnga hoga, woh aaj sahi karo. Baaki sab baad mein.

### Script 12 — "The scariest part of building"

**[HOOK]** Aaj maine database design kiya. Aur mere hisaab se ye poore project
ka sabse darawna hissa hai.

**[B-ROLL]** Schema diagram, tables connected.

Reason simple hai — **reversibility.**

UI ka colour galat hai? Do minute mein badal do. Button ki jagah galat hai?
Change kar do. Library pasand nahi aayi? Nikal do.

Lekin schema — ek baar 50 businesses ka live data aa gaya, unke payments,
unke reviews — ab column ka naam badalna ya table todna matlab **migration**.
Aur migration ke beech mein kuch toota, toh customer ka data jaa sakta hai.

**[HOOK-BACK]** Isliye maine ek framework banaya hai har decision ke liye —
**"ye reversible hai ya nahi?"**

Reversible hai — fatafat decide karo, aage badho.
Reversible nahi hai — ruko, socho, likho ki kyun.

**[CTA]** Speed accha hai. Lekin sirf wahan jahan galti sasti ho.

**[HOOK-BACK]** Isliye maine ek manual approval gate rakha hai. Customer pay kar
deta hai, lekin live nahi hota jab tak main khud check na karun ki Google listing
sahi hai.

Isse signup slow ho jaata hai. Log kehte hain friction hatao. Lekin ye friction
**jaan bujh ke** hai.

**[CTA]** Har friction buri nahi hoti. Kuch friction aapko bachati hai.

### Script 14 — "Building the screen 60% of people will quit on"

**[HOOK]** Aaj main woh screen bana raha hoon jahan mere aadhe se zyada users
chhod ke chale jaayenge. Aur mujhe pehle se pata hai.

**[B-ROLL]** The copy button, then Google's page loading.

Customer review likh chuka hai. Ab usko copy karna hai, Google pe jaana hai,
login karna hai, paste karna hai, stars phir se select karne hain, submit.

Chaar-paanch steps. Kisi aur ke app pe. Jahan mera koi control nahi.

**[HOOK-BACK]** Main isko measure bhi nahi kar sakta. Jaise hi customer Google pe
gaya — mere paas koi data nahi. Blind spot hai, aur permanent hai.

Toh maine kya kiya? Ek chhoti si screen add ki — "aage Google aayega, wahan

Naam use karo. Jo unhone mention kiya woh repeat karo. Do line bas.

**Negative review ka jawab —**
> "Sorry ki experience acha nahi raha. ___ pe call kar sakein toh theek karna
> chahenge."

Argue nahi. Excuse nahi. Offline le jao.

**[HOOK-BACK]** Ek galti jo sab karte hain — har review ka same jawab. "Thank
you for your feedback" har jagah.

Woh dikh jaata hai. Aur copy-paste dikhne se accha hai jawab na dena.

**[CTA]** Hafte mein ek baar, dus minute. Bas itna kaafi hai.

### Script 40 — "How Google decides who shows up first"

**[HOOK]** Jab koi "dentist near me" search karta hai — Google teen businesses

**[B-ROLL]** Google Maps local pack, three results highlighted.

## 12. Production notes

### Filming these

- **Vertical, 9:16.** Phone camera is fine. Natural light beats a ring light.
- **Film in batches.** 3–4 scripts per sitting. Change your shirt between takes
  so they do not look like one session.
- **Do not memorise.** Read the script twice, then talk. Small stumbles read as
  authentic; a perfect take reads as an ad.
- **Hook first, always.** Never open with "hi guys" or "so I've been building".
  The first frame is the hook.

### Captions

Burn in captions — most people watch muted. Hinglish captions in Latin script
(not Devanagari) — that is how the audience reads it.

### Which to post first

Per §10 — scripts **9**, **2**, **4**. Film together, post over a week.

### Adapting to other platforms

| Platform | Change |
|---|---|
| Instagram Reels | As written |
| YouTube Shorts | Same, add a text title card |
| LinkedIn | Cut the Hindi, keep the structure — different audience |
| Twitter/X | Turn the script into a thread; the hook is your first tweet |

### Tracking

Log what you post in [19-JOURNAL.md](19-JOURNAL.md) alongside the build. When a
video does unusually well, note **why** you think it did — that pattern is worth
more than the view count.
